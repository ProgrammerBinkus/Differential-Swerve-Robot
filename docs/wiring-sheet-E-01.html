What the check changed

Cross-checked against each part's datasheet or listing. Four items differ from the BOM as written.

Orange Pi 4 Pro header

All 40 pins as assigned. Every function comes from the 40-pin tables in the Orange Pi 4 Pro manual (v1.4). GPIO is 3.3 V and not 5 V tolerant.

Software setup

The 40-pin PWM and I2C functions are off by default. Enable these overlays (names as listed in the manual), then reboot:

i2c0 i2c1 i2c2 i2c3
pwm0 pwm2 pwm3 pwm4 pwm5 pwm6 pwm7 spwm2
  • Run all eight PWM channels at the same frequency (20 kHz is inaudible and well inside the AT8236's 0–100 kHz). Paired Allwinner channels share a clock, so mixed frequencies cause trouble.
  • Linux may number the I2C adapters differently from TWI0–3. Run i2cdetect -l once and map each adapter to its encoder in the PC app.
  • Expected scan: 0x06 on every encoder bus, plus 0x68 on I2C0 for the MPU-6050.
  • Pins 8, 10 and 12 stay free (UART7 on 8/10 is handy for a serial console).

Motor control

Each motor takes one hardware PWM pin and one direction pin. Motors are numbered clockwise from the front-left; each module pairs two motors on neighbouring edges, and each H-bridge drives motors from two different modules.

AT8236 inputs

IN1 (PWM pin)IN2 (DIR pin)Output
10Forward (OUT1 high)
01Reverse (OUT2 high)
11Brake (both low)
00Coast; sleeps after 1.2 ms

IN1 and IN2 have 100 kΩ pull-downs, so every motor coasts while the Pi boots. Logic high is 2 V minimum, so 3.3 V GPIO drives them directly.

Driving with PWM + DIR

With DIR high, the PWM pin switches between brake and reverse, so reverse speed is the time PWM is low. Invert the duty in reverse:

def set_motor(m, cmd):          # cmd from -1.0 to 1.0
    if cmd >= 0:
        m.dir.off()             # IN2 = 0
        m.pwm.duty = cmd        # drive / coast
    else:
        m.dir.on()              # IN2 = 1
        m.pwm.duty = 1 + cmd    # low time = reverse drive

Forward uses coast between pulses and reverse uses brake, so the two directions respond slightly differently at the same command. The PID loop absorbs this; keep it in mind when tuning.

Encoders and IMU

The MT6701 answers only at I2C address 0x06, so each encoder gets its own bus. The Orange Pi 4 Pro has exactly four I2C buses on the header. The MPU-6050 sits at 0x68 and shares I2C0 without conflict.

  • Power every encoder from 3.3 V. MT6701 boards put their I2C pull-ups on their own VCC, so a 5 V supply would put 5 V on the Pi's pins.
  • Leave the encoder board's OUT pad unconnected. Mount the diametric magnet on the 45T encoder gear's axis.
  • If a bus finds nothing, the board may lack pull-ups: add 4.7 kΩ from SDA and SCL to 3.3 V at the encoder end. Keep each encoder run under about 30 cm and twist SDA with GND.
  • The 2:1 gearing (90T wheel gear to 45T encoder gear) means one encoder turn equals 180° of steering. Zero within ±90° of straight before start-up, as your outline says.

Power

The battery feeds the hub, the hub's four 20 A pads feed the four H-bridges at battery voltage, and its 5 V regulator feeds the Orange Pi through the header.

First power-up

Wheels off the ground for every step.

  1. With the battery unplugged, check continuity from every GND point back to the hub's battery negative, and check that no 5 V or battery wire touches any 3.3 V or signal pin.
  2. Plug in the battery with the Orange Pi disconnected. Measure 5.0 V at the hub's 5 V pad and battery voltage at each H-bridge's VM terminal.
  3. Connect W7 and boot the Pi. Confirm 5 V between header pins 2 and 6 and 3.3 V between pins 1 and 9.
  4. Enable the overlays and run i2cdetect on each bus: one 0x06 per encoder bus and 0x68 on I2C0.
  5. Drive one motor at a time at 20% duty, forward then reverse. Confirm it matches the table, or fix the mapping in the PC app. Swap a motor's two leads if it spins the wrong way.
  6. Drive both motors of one module the same way at the same speed. The wheel should roll without steering; if it steers instead, one motor's direction is reversed.

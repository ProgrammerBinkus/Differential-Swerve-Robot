Where it sits and where it plugs in

Left: the chassis from above, front at the top, as the driver station draws it. Right: the Orange Pi's 40-pin header. Click a used pin to see where its wire goes, highlight it in the diagrams below, and set its wire color.

Each H-bridge sits between two motors that belong to two different modules. The unlabelled box under the switch is the XT60 connector.

Pin 1 is the one drawn with square corners. Find it on the board's silkscreen before counting.

Power

The battery feeds the hub through the switch. Each of the hub's four pad pairs feeds one H-bridge at battery voltage, and its 5 V regulator feeds the Orange Pi. A small strip near the Pi fans the Pi's 3.3 V and ground out to the nine boards.

Motor controllers

Four AT8236 dual H-bridges. On each channel, IN1 takes a hardware PWM pin (speed) and IN2 takes a plain GPIO (direction). Battery power comes in on VM, and each pair of outputs goes to one motor. Three channels have their two pins side by side on the header and take one 2-pin connector. The other five can't: their PWM pins sit between other PWM and ground pins, so each of their wires needs its own 1-pin connector.

Encoders and IMU

Every MT6701 answers at address 0x06, so each encoder has its own I2C bus. The MPU-6050 answers at 0x68 and shares I2C2 with encoder E3: its SCL and SDA wires splice into E3's. Bridge the "IIC" solder jumper on every encoder board: they ship in ABZ mode, where the SDA and SCL pads are A and B pulse outputs and nothing answers on I2C.

Rules for every wire

  • Wire by the label printed on the board, not by pin position. Boards sold under the same name put the same pins in different orders. The diagrams list pins by name for that reason.
  • Nothing but header pins 2 and 4 touches 5 V. The Orange Pi's pins are 3.3 V and are damaged by 5 V. Encoders, the IMU and the H-bridge VCC pins all run from the 3.3 V strip.
  • H-bridge VCC is required. The AT8236 has no logic supply pin, so on these boards VCC can only feed the chips' current-limit reference (VREF): with no VCC, that board's motors get no current. Give it 3.3 V, which with the board's 0.015 Ω sense resistors sets a limit far above the chip's own 4.2 A trip. If your board has one GND pin instead of two, land both ground wires on it.
  • Solder the 220 µF capacitor that comes with each H-bridge across its VM and GND, right at the board. The stripe marks the − lead, which goes to GND; reversed, an electrolytic capacitor can burst. It soaks up the voltage spikes the motors throw back when they brake and reverse, which otherwise land on the AT8236.
  • Motor lead polarity is free. Either lead can go to either output of its pair. Set the direction afterwards with the invert switches on the driver station's Mapping tab, or swap the two leads.
  • Bridge the encoder's "IIC" jumper (the two small pads by the arrow), then check VCC to GND does not beep. Leave Z and OUT open, and mount the magnet on the 45T encoder gear's axis, centered over the chip.
  • The encoder boards have no I2C pull-ups. The Pi's internal ones are enough for runs under 30 cm at 100 kHz (the i2c-100khz overlay). Longer runs need 4.7 kΩ from SDA and SCL to 3.3 V. The IMU board has its own, so E3's bus is covered anyway.
  • Mount the MPU-6050 flat, chip side up. The code reads only the vertical (Z) gyro. Its position on the chassis does not matter. If the heading falls when you turn the robot counter-clockwise, switch on "invert yaw" on the Mapping tab.
  • Leave AD0, INT, XDA and XCL open on the MPU-6050. AD0 open gives address 0x68, which the code expects.
  • Pin 8 is a direction pin, so leave the uart7 overlay off. Pin 8 (PL6) is also UART7 TX. Turning on UART7 would take the pin away from HB4's channel B.
  • Never plug in the Pi's USB-C supply while the hub is powering it through the header.
  • Add the suppression parts from sheet E-01: a 0.1 µF ceramic capacitor across each motor's terminals and a 470–1000 µF capacitor across the hub's battery pads.

Conductor schedule

One row per wire, in the same numbering as the diagrams. Harness tags W1 to W15 match sheet E-01 and the driver station's drawing. The last column follows the rail color; pick a color there only for a wire that differs from its rail.

After wiring

Wheels off the ground for every step.

  1. Battery unplugged: check continuity from every GND point back to the hub's BAT− pad, and check there is none between the 3.3 V strip and any 5 V or battery wire.
  2. Unplug the Pi's header wires and connect the battery. Measure 5.0 V on the hub's 5 V pad and battery voltage on each H-bridge's VM pin.
  3. Reconnect the Pi and boot it. Enable these overlays (names from the Orange Pi manual), then reboot: 
    i2c0 i2c1 i2c2 i2c3 pwm0 pwm2 pwm3 pwm4 pwm5 pwm6 pwm7 spwm2
    and the user overlay i2c-100khz (runs the encoder buses at 100 kHz). pi_setup/setup.sh does both.
  4. Run venv/bin/python -m tools.probe_hardware. It checks all 8 PWM channels and 8 direction pins, each encoder at 0x06 and the MPU-6050 at 0x68, and drives nothing.
  5. Run venv/bin/python -m tools.encoder_monitor and turn each wheel by hand. Each module's reading should change, and only its own.
  6. Run venv/bin/python -m tools.motor_test motor M1 through M8, then module FL and the other three. The tool says which invert switches to change.

Names on sheet E-01

Sheet E-01 was drawn before the front of the robot was fixed, so some of its labels differ. Motor numbers, pin numbers and harness tags are the same on both sheets.
